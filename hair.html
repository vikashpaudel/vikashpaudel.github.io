Common Hair & Scalp Concerns We Evaluate

Hair fall and scalp irritation can stem from genetics, hormones, nutritional deficiencies, stress, or inflammatory scalp conditions. Identifying the exact pattern guides safe, targeted care.

Sudden Shedding

Excessive Shedding (Telogen Effluvium)

Noticing noticeably more strands on your pillow, shower drain, or comb—often triggered 2–3 months after physical or emotional stress, high fever, childbirth, crash dieting, or iron/vitamin B12 deficiency.

Pattern Thinning

Gradual Hair Thinning (Androgenetic Alopecia)

Progressive hairline recession or crown thinning in men, and widening of the central hair part in women, driven by genetic sensitivity to hair-follicle hormones.

Focal Patches

Patchy Hair Loss (Alopecia Areata)

Smooth, round or oval coin-sized bald patches on the scalp, beard, or eyebrows caused by a localized autoimmune response affecting hair follicles.

Scalp Health

Dandruff, Itchy or Flaky Scalp

White or yellowish flakes, scalp redness, oiliness, tenderness, or scaling caused by seborrheic dermatitis, scalp psoriasis, fungal infections, or product irritation.

Other Contributing Factors: Thyroid, Hormones & Medications

Thyroid disorders, polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), post-pregnancy hormone shifts, and certain prescription drugs (such as blood pressure, arthritis, or chemotherapy medications) can also trigger diffuse shedding.

What to Expect During Your Consultation

A structured dermatological evaluation avoids guesswork and unnecessary procedures by matching treatment to your specific diagnosis.

1

Medical & Lifestyle History

Review of when shedding or scalp symptoms started, family history, recent illnesses, diet, stress levels, and current hair products or medications.

2

Scalp & Hair Follicle Examination

Close inspection of hair density, miniaturization, scalp scaling, redness, or patchy loss (and targeted blood tests such as ferritin, thyroid, or vitamin levels if indicated).

3

Individualized Care Plan

Clear discussion of realistic timelines (typically 3–6 months to judge hair cycle response), medicated shampoos or topicals, oral therapy, and whether procedural support is appropriate.

Treatment Options After Assessment

Depending on your diagnosis, Dr. Vikash Paudel may recommend medical therapy, targeted scalp care, or selected in-clinic procedures.

First-Line Medical Care

Topical & Scalp Therapies

  • Topical Minoxidil: Solution or foam applied to the scalp to support hair follicle activity in pattern thinning.
  • Medicated Shampoos & Lotions: Antifungal or anti-inflammatory scalp treatments to control dandruff, itching, and flaking.
  • Targeted Anti-Inflammatory Care: Topical or localized therapy for patchy alopecia areata.
Systemic & Nutritional Support

Oral Medications & Supplements

  • Finasteride / Anti-Androgen Therapy: Prescription options for eligible men or women with hormonally mediated pattern hair thinning.
  • Low-Dose Oral Minoxidil (0.625–2.5 mg): An off-label medical option for selected patients—such as those who experience scalp irritation from topical solutions—used strictly under doctor supervision.
  • Targeted Nutritional Correction: Addressing confirmed iron, vitamin D, vitamin B12, or protein deficiencies.
Option for Selected Patients

PRP (Platelet-Rich Plasma) Therapy

An adjunct option for selected patients after dermatological assessment. A small sample of the patient’s own blood is processed to concentrate platelets and injected into thinning areas of the scalp to support existing follicles alongside medical care. Individual response varies and regrowth cannot be guaranteed.

Option for Selected Patients

GFC (Growth Factor Concentrate)

A refined, acellular preparation derived from a patient’s blood sample that isolates specific platelet growth factors for targeted scalp application. Considered for selected candidates with early-to-moderate thinning following clinical evaluation, rather than as a standalone guarantee.

Surgical & Emerging Therapies: Hair Transplant & Regenerative Research

For stabilized, advanced pattern hair loss with adequate donor hair, follicular unit hair transplantation (FUE/FUT) may be discussed after medical stabilization. Investigational approaches such as stem-cell-derived therapies remain under clinical evaluation and require careful specialist guidance.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Answers to common patient questions about hair fall timelines, PRP/GFC suitability, and oral medications.

Hair follicles grow in slow cycles. Reduced daily shedding often begins after 6 to 8 weeks of consistent treatment, while visible improvements in hair density typically take 3 to 6 months. Patience and regular follow-up are essential.

PRP concentrates platelets from your blood plasma, whereas GFC uses a specialized kit to isolate acellular growth factors before scalp injection. Both are offered only to selected patients after clinical assessment—most commonly alongside foundational medical therapy for early-to-moderate thinning—and results vary by individual.

Clinical studies show that low-dose oral minoxidil (typically 0.625 mg to 2.5 mg daily) is generally well tolerated in appropriately screened patients, especially those who develop scalp irritation from topical minoxidil. Possible side effects include mild fluid retention, lightheadedness, or unwanted facial/body hair, so it must only be taken under a physician's prescription and monitoring.
