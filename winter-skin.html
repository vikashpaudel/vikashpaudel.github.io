Winter Skin Care

Cold air, low humidity, and indoor heating draw moisture out of your skin barrier. Use this practical guide from Consultant Dermatologist Dr. Vikash Paudel (MD Dermatology, NMC #9111) to protect your skin daily and manage dry, itchy, or cracked skin.

Everyday Winter Skin Care Routine

Small adjustments to how you wash, moisturize, and dress prevent most cold-weather dryness and itching.

Step 1 · Gentle Cleansing

Keep Showers Short & Lukewarm

Very hot water strips protective surface lipids quickly, leaving skin tighter and itchier once you step out.

  • Limit baths or showers to 5–10 minutes using lukewarm water.
  • Use mild, fragrance-free, soap-free cleansers instead of harsh bar soaps.
  • Pat skin gently with a soft towel—leave it slightly damp before moisturizing.
Step 2 · Seal in Moisture

Switch to Creams or Ointments

Thin pump lotions evaporate rapidly in dry winter air. Thicker creams and ointments trap water inside the skin barrier.

  • Apply moisturizer within 3 minutes of bathing and after every hand wash.
  • Choose formulas with ceramides, glycerin, hyaluronic acid, shea butter, or petrolatum.
  • Continue daily broad-spectrum sunscreen (SPF 30+), especially in sunny winter afternoons.
Step 3 · Clothing & Indoor Air

Layer Soft Fabrics & Humidify

Direct contact with rough wool and dry heated rooms can trigger itching even on healthy skin.

  • Wear a soft, breathable cotton layer underneath wool or synthetic sweaters.
  • Wear insulated gloves outdoors and waterproof gloves when washing dishes.
  • Use a room humidifier (aim for 40–50% humidity) when running indoor heaters.

Common Winter Skin Concerns

Recognize why specific areas—such as shins, lips, hands, heels, and eczema-prone patches—react to cold weather and how to care for them.

Xerosis

Dry, Flaky & Itchy Skin (Winter Itch)

Rough, scaly, or tight skin—most common on the lower legs, arms, and torso—caused by moisture loss and reduced natural skin oils. Severe dryness can develop fine, cracked lines.

Practical care: Apply a thick fragrance-free cream or ointment twice daily, especially right after bathing.
Cheilitis

Chapped & Cracked Lips

Lips lack oil glands and dry out faster than facial skin. Licking dry lips temporarily wets them but worsens cracking as saliva evaporates.

Practical care: Use a plain petrolatum or ceramide lip balm frequently and before bed; avoid flavored or menthol balms that irritate.
Fissures

Cracked Hands & Split Heels

Frequent handwashing, cold wind, and walking barefoot on cold floors strip protective lipids, leading to painful cracks over knuckles, fingertips, and heels.

Practical care: Apply thick hand/heel ointment (such as petrolatum or urea cream) at night and cover with cotton gloves or socks.
Barrier Flares

Eczema & Psoriasis Flare-Ups

Low humidity and reduced winter sunlight weaken the skin barrier, triggering red, itchy eczema patches or thicker, scaly psoriasis plaques.

Practical care: Moisturize generously multiple times daily and use prescribed anti-inflammatory creams early rather than over-the-counter steroid mixes.
Cold Circulation

Chilblains (Pernio)

Red or purplish, tender, itchy, or burning bumps on fingers, toes, or ears caused by small blood vessels reacting to cold followed by rapid warming.

Practical care: Keep extremities warmly covered and rewarm skin gradually—never place cold hands or feet directly on a heater or in hot water.
Cold Reactions

Cold Urticaria & Frostnip

Cold urticaria causes itchy hives or swelling minutes after cold wind or cold water exposure. High-altitude trekkers also face frostnip (numb, pale skin) in freezing temperatures.

Practical care: Dress in windproof layers and seek medical evaluation for antihistamine guidance or urgent care if skin becomes numb, hard, or blistered.

When to See a Dermatologist

Home moisturizing resolves mild winter dryness, but book a medical evaluation if you notice any of the following signs:

  • Dryness, redness, or itching persists or worsens after 1–2 weeks of regular moisturizing.
  • Deep, bleeding, or painful cracks develop on your hands, fingers, lips, or heels.
  • Signs of skin infection appear—such as yellow crusting, oozing, pus, spreading warmth, or increasing swelling.
  • Itching disrupts your sleep or daily activities, or pre-existing eczema or psoriasis flares.
  • Painful purple-red chilblains, blisters, or cold-triggered hives/lip swelling occur.
  • Seek urgent emergency care if cold-exposed skin turns pale, hard, and numb (possible frostbite) or if hives are accompanied by dizziness or trouble breathing.
