Cosmetic Dermatology & Aesthetic Treatment Categories

Well-Established Evidence Offered (Confirm Clinic)

A. Chemical Peeling

Controlled medical exfoliation using glycolic, salicylic, mandelic, lactic, Jessner's, TCA, or combination peels (superficial to medium-depth) for acne, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, melasma, photoageing, and mild texture irregularity.

Standardized Procedure Details

Contraindications: Active skin infection/herpes, open wounds, recent sunburn, pregnancy (agent-specific).

Duration & Downtime: Brief in-clinic session; downtime ranges from minimal flaking to several days of peeling depending on depth.

Risks: Temporary redness, stinging, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (especially without sun protection). Avoid unsupervised home acid peeling.

Chemical Peel Guide Consult

B. Laser & Energy-Based Dermatology

Covers true lasers (Fractional CO₂, Erbium:YAG, Q-switched Nd:YAG, Picosecond, Long-pulsed Nd:YAG, Diode, Alexandrite) and non-laser energy devices (IPL, Radiofrequency, MNRF, HIFU) for scars, unwanted hair, selected pigment/vascular lesions, tattoos, and resurfacing.

Standardized Procedure Details

Risks & Safety: Burns, dyspigmentation, scarring, eye injury (strict ocular shielding required), and paradoxical hypertrichosis. Requires operator expertise and skin-phototype-appropriate wavelengths.

Downtime: Minimal (non-ablative/hair reduction) to moderate/longer (ablative fractional resurfacing).

Laser Guide Consult
Well-Established Evidence Offered (Confirm Clinic)

C & D. Acne Scars & Microneedling (MNRF)

Individualized combination care for ice-pick, rolling, boxcar, hypertrophic, and keloidal scars using subcision, conventional/automated microneedling, radiofrequency microneedling (adds thermal dermal remodeling), fractional lasers, TCA CROSS, punch techniques, and selected fillers.

Standardized Procedure Details

Key Distinction: Post-inflammatory redness (PIE) or dark marks (PIH) are flat color changes—not true atrophic scars—and are treated differently.

Safety & Expectations: Sterile single-use cartridges are mandatory; avoid home dermarollers. Multiple sessions are usually needed and complete scar elimination cannot be guaranteed.

Acne Guide Consult
Well-Established Evidence Offered (Confirm Clinic)

E. Botulinum Toxin

Neuromodulator injections that temporarily relax targeted muscles for approved indications (glabellar lines, forehead lines, crow's feet, primary axillary hyperhidrosis) and selected off-label uses (masseter hypertrophy, facial contouring).

Standardized Procedure Details

Course & Duration: Effects develop over days and are temporary (typically several months), requiring periodic maintenance if desired.

Risks: Bruising, headache, facial asymmetry, eyelid ptosis, unwanted muscle weakness, and rare systemic effects. Contraindicated in pregnancy, breastfeeding, and certain neuromuscular disorders.

Well-Established Evidence Offered (Confirm Clinic)

F. Dermal Fillers & Facial Volumization

Temporary hyaluronic acid (HA) and longer-lasting fillers for facial volume restoration, lip contouring, selected nasolabial folds, tear troughs, chin/jawline balance, and selected atrophic scars.

Standardized Procedure Details & Safety

Safety Statement: Dermal fillers are medical procedures requiring advanced anatomical training and emergency preparedness. Risks include swelling, bruising, nodules, infection, delayed inflammation, vascular occlusion, skin necrosis, and rare vision-threatening complications.

Reversibility: Hyaluronidase may be used by trained physicians for selected HA filler complications.

Moderate / Variable Evidence Offered (Confirm Clinic)

G & H. PRP, PRF & Hair Restoration

Autologous platelet-rich plasma (PRP) and platelet-rich fibrin (PRF) used alongside medical therapy, low-level light therapy, or hair transplantation (FUE/FUT) for androgenetic alopecia and adjunctive acne scar care.

Standardized Procedure Details

Evidence & Diagnosis: PRP has moderate supporting evidence in pattern hair loss (with protocol variability) and limited evidence for general skin rejuvenation. It is not a guaranteed permanent cure. Inflammatory or scarring alopecia requires medical treatment before any transplant.

I & J. Melasma Care & Laser Hair Reduction

Pigmentation: Medical depigmenting therapy, photoprotection, gentle peels, and selected pigment lasers (lasers are not a permanent cure for melasma and carry rebound risk). Laser Hair Reduction: Multiple sessions for long-term hair reduction on face/body, hirsutism, and pseudofolliculitis barbae.

Standardized Procedure Details

Hormonal Evaluation: Underlying hormonal causes (e.g., PCOS) should be assessed when clinically indicated for facial hirsutism. Maintenance sessions may be needed.

K, L & M. Photoageing, Dermatosurgery & Keloids

Healthy-ageing rejuvenation, safe removal of verified benign growths (skin tags, seborrheic keratoses, milia, syringomas, xanthelasma, cysts, cherry angiomas) via electrosurgery, RF ablation, cryotherapy, or excision, plus multimodal scar, keloid, and stretch mark care.

Safety Before Aesthetics

Aesthetic Dermatology for Nepalese Skin & Patient Safety

Nepalese & South Asian Skin Phototypes

  • Patients in Nepal span a wide spectrum of Fitzpatrick skin phototypes (commonly III–V, with individual variation)—never a single uniform skin type.
  • Higher epidermal melanin increases susceptibility to post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH), melasma rebound, and keloid/hypertrophic scarring after overly aggressive procedures.
  • Conservative settings, appropriate laser wavelengths (such as longer wavelengths when indicated), strict broad-spectrum photoprotection against high-altitude UV, and dermatologist-led assessment minimize risk.

Contraindications & Isotretinoin Update

  • Suitability is procedure-specific and evaluated against active bacterial/viral/fungal infection (including herpes simplex), pregnancy/breastfeeding, keloid history, uncontrolled diabetes, immunosuppression, bleeding disorders, anticoagulants, recent sunburn, and unrealistic expectations.
  • Contemporary Isotretinoin Evidence: Modern dermatologic surgery consensus no longer applies a universal 6-month delay to all cosmetic procedures; superficial peels, manual dermabrasion/subcision, and selected non-ablative lasers may be considered individually, while mechanical dermabrasion and fully ablative resurfacing remain cautioned.
  • Never discontinue prescribed medications without your treating physician's supervision.

Post-Procedure Warning Signs Requiring Urgent or Emergency Care

While mild redness, temporary swelling, dryness, or light bruising can be expected after selected procedures, seek urgent in-person medical assessment for severe or increasing pain, rapidly spreading redness, fever, pus, extensive blistering, or dusky/pale skin after filler injection. Sudden visual disturbance or severe eye pain following facial filler injection is a medical emergency—go to the nearest hospital emergency department immediately. Online consultation is never sufficient for serious procedural complications.

Evidence vs. Misconceptions

Cosmetic Dermatology Myths and Facts

Myth

Cosmetic procedures are completely risk-free.

Fact

Every medical procedure carries potential risks; specialist assessment minimizes but never eliminates them.

Myth

Chemical peels permanently thin the skin.

Fact

Appropriately spaced superficial peels remove dull surface cells and can support healthy dermal remodeling.

Myth

Lasers cannot be used on darker skin.

Fact

Selected wavelengths and conservative settings can be used safely on darker phototypes under dermatologist care.

Myth

Melasma is permanently cured by lasers.

Fact

Melasma is chronic and prone to recurrence; lasers can even worsen it if used aggressively.

Myth

Botulinum toxin and fillers are the same.

Fact

Botulinum toxin relaxes muscles for dynamic lines; fillers restore volume or contour structure.

Myth

PRP guarantees complete hair regrowth.

Fact

Responses vary; PRP is an adjunct that may improve density in selected patients, not a guaranteed cure.

Myth

One procedure treats all acne scars.

Fact

Ice-pick, rolling, boxcar, and raised scars each respond best to different combined modalities.

Myth

Laser hair reduction is 100% lifelong removal.

Fact

It provides long-term hair reduction; hormonal factors and maintenance sessions still apply.

Myth

More aggressive treatment gives better results.

Fact

Overly aggressive settings significantly raise the risk of burns, scarring, and pigmentation in South Asian skin.

Myth

Procedures replace daily skincare.

Fact

Daily sunscreen, gentle cleansing, and moisturizing remain essential to maintain any procedural result.

Myth

All natural or regenerative therapies are safe.

Fact

Unapproved exosomes, unregulated injectables, and harsh botanicals can cause infection or allergic reactions.

Myth

Anyone can safely perform cosmetic injections.

Fact

Injectables require medical training, vascular anatomy mastery, and emergency complication protocols.
